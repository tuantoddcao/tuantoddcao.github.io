My teaching

Teaching philosophy

The classroom is where I meet people. I feel fortunate to have met talented and passionate students who have taught me as much as I have taught them. I aim to create a student-centered and industry-connected learning environment where students actively construct knowledge through discussion, collaboration, and reflection. My teaching follows an interactive learning approach using three classroom formats: L–C (Lecturer–Class Reflection), L–I–I–C (Lecturer–Individual–Individual–Class Reflection), and L–G–G–C (Lecturer–Group–Group–Class Reflection). Rather than relying on conventional group presentations, students engage in simulations of real business activities to apply concepts in authentic contexts. I also integrate industry perspectives by inviting experts to share their experiences and discuss current business challenges with students.

Interactive Game: What Reputation Did You Build?

This is an example of how I use interactive activities to turn communication theories into decision-making exercises. Students take the role of a management team, respond to a series of business situations, and see how their decisions shape stakeholder perceptions over time. The activity helps students explore the difference between corporate identity—how an organization wants to be seen—and corporate reputation—how stakeholders actually see it.

CORPORATE COMMUNICATIONS

What Reputation Did You Build?

You are part of the management team of Nadia, a new consumer electronics company.

NADIA'S IDENTITY
Human Responsible Innovative

Nadia wants people to see the company this way. But reputation is not created by words alone.

Your team will face 6 situations. Choose what Nadia should do in each situation.

There is no perfect answer. Make the decision you think is best for the company.
